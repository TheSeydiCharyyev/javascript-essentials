Day 18. Tasks

Lessons 2 and 3, 90 minutes. Warm-up — 20 minutes, main tasks — 50 minutes, starred tasks — 20 minutes. Tasks 1–3 are done in the console, the rest in the folder day-18.

Warm-up

1. Twelve cells
Easy6 min

see the four kinds of rounding side by side, including on negatives.

Fill in the table: three numbers through four ways. Predict first, then check.

const values = [4.5, -4.1, -4.5];

// for each one: Math.round, Math.floor, Math.ceil, Math.trunc
Two cells will almost certainly differ from your prediction, and both are in the rows with a minus. Think about what “down” means for a negative number.
  • all twelve cells are filled in;
  • both rows where floor and trunc differed are found;
  • it is explained why Math.round(-4.5) gives −4, not −5;
  • it is written down which method you will take to “just drop the kopecks”.
2. Predict first: max and min
Easy7 min

catch three unexpected answers.

Math.max(3, 7, 5);
Math.min(3, 7, 5);
Math.max();
Math.min();
Math.max(3, NaN, 5);
Math.max([3, 7, 5]);
The last four answers will surprise you. For the two with arguments the cause is the same: the function got something other than you thought. For the two empty calls it is in what Math.max and Math.min start counting from.
  • six predictions are written down before checking;
  • it is explained why Math.max() gives -Infinity;
  • it is explained why an array gives NaN;
  • a loop is written that finds the maximum of an array through Math.max;
  • it is checked that the loop works on an array of one element.
3. Rolling a die
Easy7 min

write the formula of a random integer and check its boundaries.

Write randomInt(min, max) and make 20 rolls of randomInt(1, 6). Then answer three questions, checking them with code:

  • does 0 or 7 ever come out;
  • do fractional values come out;
  • what randomInt(5, 5) returns.
Twenty rolls are too few to see a skew, but enough to catch going out of bounds. To check the boundaries make a loop of 10,000 rolls with an “out of range” counter.
  • the formula is written down and works;
  • over 10,000 rolls the “out of range” counter stayed at zero;
  • it is written down what randomInt(5, 5) returned and why;
  • it is explained why the formula has + 1.

Main tasks

4. Rounding to a step
Medium12 min

master the technique “divide, round, multiply”.

The price is 1234.567. Get:

  • to integers, to tens, to hundreds;
  • down to a multiple of five;
  • up to a multiple of fifty;
  • to two digits after the point — as a number, not a string.

Then write roundTo(value, step), which does all this in one function, and check it on all six cases.

The last case is the step 0.01. On 1234.567 it will come out right, but roundTo(0.7, 0.1) will give an unexpected tail — look at what happens when multiplying by a fractional step, and remember yesterday.
  • all six values are obtained and written down;
  • roundTo works with the steps 1, 10, 100, 5, 50;
  • for the step 0.01 and for roundTo(0.7, 0.1) it is written down what came out, and it is explained why;
  • it is shown how the result differs from toFixed(2) — with typeof;
  • a parameter “how to round” with a default value is added.
5. A frequency counter
Medium13 min

prove with a counter that there is an error, although the program works.

Compare two formulas over 60,000 rolls each, counting how many times each outcome came out:

Math.round(Math.random() * 6);              // version A
Math.floor(Math.random() * 6) + 1;          // version B
It is handy to count in an object: counts[n] = (counts[n] || 0) + 1. Compare not the exact numbers — they are different for everyone — but the ratio between the edge and the middle bars.
  • both frequency tables are obtained and written down;
  • it is named how many outcomes each version has;
  • it is written down roughly how many times less often the edges of version A come out than the middle;
  • the cause is explained through the scheme from section 8 of the lecture;
  • it is written down why such an error is not visible on 20 rolls.
6. Guess the number
Medium12 min

build a game and check it without a single click.

Write checkGuess(guess, secret), which answers “higher”, “lower”, “you got it!” or “this is not an integer”. Check all four branches.

Then write stepsToGuess(secret), which plays against itself, naming the middle of the remaining segment each time, and returns the number of moves.

The middle of the segment is Math.floor((low + high) / 2). After each move one of the ends moves: if the middle is less than the secret number, the new lower end is mid + 1.
  • all four branches of checkGuess are checked with calls;
  • stepsToGuess works for all numbers from 1 to 100;
  • the worst case is found — how many moves and for which numbers;
  • it is written down how many moves are needed for the range 1…1000, and it is checked with code;
  • it is explained why the number of moves is about how many times the number can be divided in half.
7. The “Report card”: min, max and percents
Medium13 min

add three columns to the report using today’s methods.

Copy the folder day-17 into day-18 and improve it:

  • maxOf and minOf — with a loop through Math.max and Math.min;
  • the columns “Min” and “Max” in the header and in the rows;
  • the column “%” — the share of valid marks, rounded with Math.round;
  • a line of the right length under the header.
  • the averages match yesterday’s;
  • Aman has 75 in the “%” column, Oguljan has 67;
  • the columns are even for all five students;
  • it is explained why the rounding stands after the division, not inside it;
  • it is written down what maxOf([]) returns, and it is decided whether a check is needed.

Starred tasks

8. Geometry on the page
Hard10 min

combine four methods in one task.

Write three functions and check them on the data from the example:

circleArea(5);              // 78.54   the area of a circle, to hundredths
circleLength(5);            // 31.42   the length of a circle
distance(0, 0, 3, 4);       // 5       the distance between points
distance(1, 1, 4, 5);       // 5

Round the results to hundredths as a number, not a string.

For the distance there is the ready Math.hypot — it is given the differences of the coordinates. Rounding to hundredths as a number is the technique from section 4 of the lecture.
  • all four calls give what is written;
  • the rounding returns a number — checked with typeof;
  • a shared function for rounding to hundredths is moved out;
  • a function “the sine of an angle in degrees” is added and checked on 0, 30, 90;
  • for 30 degrees the exact result before rounding is written down, and it is explained where the nines came from.
9. Five catches
Hard10 min

bring together everything that behaves unexpectedly today.

For each fragment first write down a prediction, then check and explain the difference in one sentence.

// 1
console.log(Math.floor(-4.1), Math.trunc(-4.1));

// 2
const marks = [3, 5, 4];
console.log(Math.max(marks));

// 3
console.log(Math.sin(90));

// 4
console.log(Math.round(4.6).toFixed(2));

// 5
console.log(typeof Math.sumPrecise);
Not one fragment crashes — all five quietly give something other than it seems. The fifth depends on where you run it: in the browser and in Node the answer may differ.
  • the predictions are written down before running;
  • for all five it is written down what was printed, and the cause is named;
  • fragments 1–4 are rewritten correctly;
  • for fragment 5 it is written down what you got, and a conclusion is drawn whether this method can be used;
  • it is written down which of the five catches is the most dangerous in a real program and why.
